Soap Bubbles: \[\frac{\cosh(\mu)}{\mu} = c\]

The equation \(\frac{\cosh(\mu)}{\mu} = c\) arises when finding the shape of a soap film stretched between two parallel circular rings. The surface that minimizes area is called a catenoid.

If the rings have radius \(R\) and are separated by a distance \(2a\), the profile of the soap film is given by \(y(x) = k \cosh\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\). Applying the boundary condition \(y(a) = R\) gives \(R = k \cosh\left(\frac{a}{k}\right)\). By letting \(\mu = \frac{a}{k}\) and the separation ratio \(c = \frac{R}{a}\), we obtain \(\frac{\cosh(\mu)}{\mu} = c.\)

Mathematical Properties

  • The function \(f(\mu) = \frac{\cosh(\mu)}{\mu}\) is odd: \(f(-\mu) = -f(\mu)\).
  • For \(\mu > 0\), it has a global minimum where \(\mu \tanh(\mu) = 1\). This stationary point occurs at \(\mu_0 \approx 1.199678\).
  • The minimum value at this point is \(c_0 = \frac{\cosh(\mu_0)}{\mu_0} \approx 1.508879\).

(Note that in our first look at soap bubbles we took \(R=1\), so \(H_{\text{crit}} = \frac{2}{c_0} = \frac{2}{1.508879} \approx 1.325\).)

Physical Interpretation

By Value of \(\mathbf{c > 0}\):

  • \(c > 1.508879\): 2 catenoid solutions (a "deep" and "shallow" catenoid, though only one is stable)
  • \(c = 1.508879\): 1 catenoid solution (critical separation)
  • \(c < 1.508879\): 0 catenoid solutions (the film ruptures into two separate disks, known as the Goldschmidt solution)
