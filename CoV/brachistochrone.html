Brachistochrone: \[\frac{\theta_f - \sin\theta_f}{1 - \cos\theta_f} = c\]

The Brachistochrone problem asks for the shape of the curve between two points \( (0,0) \) and \( (a,h) \) that a mass will slide down under gravity in the shortest possible time. (Note: here \(y\) is measured downwards).

The solution is a cycloid, parameterized as: \[ x(\theta) = r(\theta - \sin\theta) \] \[ y(\theta) = r(1 - \cos\theta) \] where \(\theta\) is the parameter. At the endpoint \( (a,h) \), the parameter reaches a final value \(\theta_f\), satisfying: \[ \frac{x}{y} = \frac{\theta_f - \sin\theta_f}{1 - \cos\theta_f} = \frac{a}{h} = c \]

Mathematical Properties

  • The function \(f(\theta_f) = \frac{\theta_f - \sin\theta_f}{1 - \cos\theta_f}\) is strictly increasing for \(\theta_f \in (0, 2\pi)\).
  • We have \(\lim_{\theta_f \to 0} f(\theta_f) = 0\).
  • Also \(\lim_{\theta_f \to 2\pi} f(\theta_f) = \infty\).
  • For any \(c > 0\), there is exactly one solution \(\theta_f \in (0, 2\pi)\).

Physical Interpretation

The value \(\theta_f\) represents the total "swing" angle of the rolling circle to reach the endpoint. If \(\theta_f < \pi\), the particle is still descending on the first half of the cycloid arc. If \(\theta_f=\pi\), the particle is at the very bottom of the cycloid arc. If \(\theta_f> \pi\), the particle has passed the minimum and is ascending to its final position.
